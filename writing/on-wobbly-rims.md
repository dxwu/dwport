There is a tea bowl on my shelf whose rim is not level. I noticed it as soon as I lifted it off the wheel, and I had about four seconds to decide whether to fix it.

I didn't. This is a short essay about why.

## The four seconds

When a pot comes off the wheel it is at its most fixable and its most fragile. Push too hard and it collapses. Wait too long and it stiffens into whatever it is. The window for "just one more adjustment" is real but short, and it is almost always shorter than you think.

Software has this window too. It's the time between "it works" and "it shipped." Everything you touch in that window has a nonzero chance of collapsing the pot.

## What the rim is for

A rim is for lips. That's the whole job. The bowl holds tea, the rim meets your mouth, and if both of those things happen, the pot has succeeded.

> Perfectly level is a property of the maker's ego, not of the tea.

I still fix rims. Most of the time. But I try to ask first who the fix is for.
